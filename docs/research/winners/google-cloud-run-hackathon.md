## Per-winner notes

### 1. Storytopia (Grand Prize). Deep-dived: no (repo read fully; Devpost via search excerpts; video link found but blocked)

- **What it does:** A child draws a character on a canvas (iPad or computer), or uploads a photo of a toy or drawing. The app turns it into a cartoon character, the child or a parent picks a lesson theme ("kindness, online safety, or learning to ride a bike"), and the app produces "a narrated, illustrated 8-scene quest". Each scene has a story segment, a question, and one correct and one incorrect answer ([README](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia/blob/main/README.md)). The hero image shows an example: "Bob's Safe Surfing Adventure". A burger character gets a pop-up asking for his name and address, and the child picks "Ask a grown-up for help" or "Type in his name and address right away!" ([Storytopia-Main.png](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia/blob/main/Storytopia-Main.png)).
- **Sponsor tools and depth:** two Cloud Run services (Next.js front end, FastAPI plus ADK back end). Three ADK `LlmAgent`s: Visionizer (Gemini 2.0 Flash vision plus Imagen 3.0, with an age-appropriateness check), Quest Creator (Gemini 2.0 Flash) and Illustrator (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, fed the character image for consistency). Also Cloud Storage and Cloud TTS / Gemini TTS narration ([README](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia/blob/main/README.md), [visionizer.py](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia/blob/main/agents_service/agents/visionizer.py)). ADK use is real but moderate. Each agent runs through an ADK `Runner` with `InMemorySessionService`, and the FastAPI endpoints call the agents one after another. It does not use ADK's `SequentialAgent` ([main.py](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia/blob/main/agents_service/main.py)). It earns both bonus items I can check: Google AI models and multiple Cloud Run services.
- **Autonomy:** generates content when the user clicks. It takes no actions outside the app. The human picks the lesson and answers the questions.
- **Demo style and video:** the README leads with a hero image, an architecture diagram and three GIFs (character creation, lesson choice, a quest), and it says "Demo video and GIF examples are sped up for presentation purposes." It also gives honest runtimes: "Character generation: ~15 seconds", "Quest generation: ~2.5 minutes" ([README](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia/blob/main/README.md)). The team's later repo links "our demo video": https://youtu.be/UMhJqCjEJbk ([Storytopia-LLMObs README](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia-LLMObs)). YouTube is blocked, so title, length and opening are unknown (unverified).
- **Human or emotional story:** a problem framed around kids, with no named person. Devpost: "Kids spend more time than ever in front of screens - but not enough time using them to create. Passive content consumption makes them audiences, not authors of their own ideas." ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/storytopia-bu917p), search excerpt, unverified wording). Google's results post repeats the frame: "make screen time more engaging and meaningful" ([@googledevs](https://x.com/googledevs/status/2013671669743997321), search excerpt, unverified wording).
- **Live URL:** https://storytopia-frontend-700174635185.us-central1.run.app (listed in README; blocked here, so not tested).
- **Team:** 2. Commit authors are Harpita-P and Harpith2. The visible history runs Nov 8 to 11, 2025, and includes commits named "Devpost Thumbnail" and "Project Architecture" ([commits](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia/commits/main)).
- **After the win:** the same team added Datadog LLM Observability to Storytopia, with 5 custom evaluation signals and 7 alert monitors, and the repo says it took "Winner (2nd Place) of the Datadog x Google AI Partner Hackathon 2025" ([Storytopia-LLMObs](https://github.com/Harpita-P/Storytopia-LLMObs)).
- **Why it most likely won (my reading):** judges understand the problem in one sentence, and the payoff is visual (a child's drawing comes to life). It has a clear three-agent pipeline with named roles and a clean architecture diagram (the Demo and Presentation criterion asks for one). It uses several Google models, ships two Cloud Run services and a live URL, and builds child safety into the first agent. It would also have fit the AI Agents category.

### 2. Rehearsal (Best of AI Studio). Deep-dived: no (repo read; Devpost page not found; video unknown)

- **Repo match:** [konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon), About: "Street View + AI = Your safe space to mess up in 37 languages." The name and AI Studio build match the winner, but I found no Devpost link that confirms it (unverified).
- **What it does:** you drag Google Street View's Pegman anywhere on a map ("Drag me anywhere to start rehearsal!"). The app creates a scene for that place (cafe, market, station) with an AI character who has a name, voice, fluency level, patience, accent and a quirk. You then talk to it out loud in real time. If you slip into English, a bilingual character teaches you the phrase in its language and then answers. A character who speaks no English acts confused, slows down, and "shows" pictures. At the end you get "what went well", "what to try next" and an "immersion score". It also offers hints, translation, study cards and spoken audio ([metadata.json](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/blob/main/metadata.json), [types.ts](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/blob/main/types.ts), [geminiService.ts](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/blob/main/services/geminiService.ts)).
- **Sponsor tools and depth:** built in Google AI Studio (the README points to an AI Studio app, and the repo looks like an AI Studio export: one large first commit plus `metadata.json` asking for microphone access). Models: Gemini Live native audio (`gemini-2.5-flash-native-audio-preview-09-2025`) with Google Search grounding, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini TTS (`gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts`), Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, plus Google Maps / Street View. An Express back end on Cloud Run (europe-west1) keeps the API keys off the client ([server.js](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/blob/main/backend/server.js)). The Gemini feature set is used heavily. The Cloud Run part is thin (an API proxy).
- **Autonomy:** a conversation partner. It responds and coaches, and takes no actions.
- **Demo style and video:** cover image of the Pegman grabbing a "Rehearse Here" button ([3.png](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/blob/main/3.png)). Video unknown (unverified).
- **Human or emotional story:** a feeling rather than a person: "Your safe space to mess up in 37 languages" (repo About). No named person found.
- **Live URL:** AI Studio app https://ai.studio/apps/drive/15jaq3BOtqgpAOcydFIbdhyxrm4WynNsu ([README](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/blob/main/README.md)). Back end https://rehearsal-backend-115258387751.europe-west1.run.app ([geminiService.ts](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/blob/main/services/geminiService.ts)). Neither was tested.
- **Team:** 1 visible committer (konxu). All 5 commits are dated Nov 9, 2025 ([commits](https://github.com/konxu/Rehearsal-for-Cloud-Run-Hackathon/commits/main)).
- **Why it most likely won (my reading):** it shows off the most Gemini abilities in one simple experience (live voice, images, speech, search grounding) and uses Google's own Street View as the stage. Anyone can grasp the idea in seconds, and it fits the AI Studio "vibe-code then deploy" brief.

### 3. PermitScan AI (Best of AI Agents). Deep-dived: no (repo read; Devpost write-up read via a copy in the repo; video unknown)

- **What it does:** "PermitScan AI automates hazard discovery → permit creation → permit validation → permit refinement for oil & gas Work Orders (WOs)." ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/permitflow-ai), search excerpt, unverified wording). Given a work order ID, agent A1 finds hazards (Gemini 2.5 Pro, with RAG over past incidents and permits plus a weather tool). A2 drafts permits (Gemini 2.5 Flash, policy templates). A3 checks them against deterministic compliance rules (Gemini 2.5 Pro). A4 fixes what failed (Gemini 2.5 Flash), and A3 and A4 loop up to 2 times. Each permit links to the incidents it was based on ([README](https://github.com/sagban/permitflowAI/blob/main/README.md), [HACKATHON_SUBMISSION.md](https://github.com/sagban/permitflowAI/blob/main/docs/HACKATHON_SUBMISSION.md)). The repo's submission file has Devpost's sections (Inspiration, What it does, How we built it...) and images hosted on Devpost's CDN, so it is very likely the Devpost text.
- **Sponsor tools and depth:** deep. It uses ADK `SequentialAgent` plus `LoopAgent` (verified in [agent.py](https://github.com/sagban/permitflowAI/blob/main/sequential-agent/agent.py)), Pydantic output schemas, and state passed between agents with `output_key`. Other pieces: Vertex AI RAG Engine, Gemini 2.5 Pro and Flash, the Google Maps Platform Weather API, an agent service deployed with `adk deploy cloud_run`, and a React front end as a second Cloud Run service. The write-up also describes two Cloud Run Jobs plus Cloud Scheduler and Pub/Sub for refreshing the RAG data. The repo shows only a `setup_rag_corpus.py` script for this, and the write-up admits "Current demo uses localStorage and JSON files", so the jobs are unverified.
- **Autonomy:** acts with approval. It drafts permits and checks them itself, then a person reviews them and clicks "Approve" ("if validation passes") ([README](https://github.com/sagban/permitflowAI/blob/main/README.md)).
- **Demo style and video:** the README walks through the screens (dashboard, 4-step progress bar, Details, Validation and Evidence tabs). Video unknown (unverified).
- **Human or emotional story:** stakes and statistics, with no named person: "Industrial safety in oil & gas operations is a matter of life and death." The write-up then lists figures such as "1,075 workplace fatalities" and claims "4-8 hours → 2-5 minutes" ([HACKATHON_SUBMISSION.md](https://github.com/sagban/permitflowAI/blob/main/docs/HACKATHON_SUBMISSION.md)). These figures are self-reported and mostly unsourced.
- **Live URL:** https://permitflow-frontend-624881849547.us-central1.run.app/ (in README; not tested).
- **Team:** 1 visible committer (sagban). The 14 commits are dated Nov 9 to 10, 2025 ([commits](https://github.com/sagban/permitflowAI/commits/main)). The author also wrote a Medium post, "From Idea to Deployment: How I built PermitFlow AI for the Google Cloud Run Hackathon" ([Medium](https://sagbansal.medium.com/from-idea-to-deployment-how-i-built-permitflow-ai-for-the-google-cloud-run-hackathon-3a0fa09b6f39), blocked; title from search). That post probably earned the blog bonus (unverified).
- **Why it most likely won (my reading):** a clear use of the category's required feature (4 ADK agents with a real loop). It also has enterprise stakes (deaths, money), deterministic checks plus cited evidence to earn trust, a human approval step, several Cloud Run resources, and a blog post.

### 4. Commissure (Best of GPUs). Deep-dived: no (repo read; Devpost tagline via excerpt; video unknown)

- **What it does:** "Commissure turns Cloud Run into an HPC-grade LLM runtime - slicing giant models into GPU microservices and streaming activations over gRPC, pushing serverless beyond its limits." ([GitHub About](https://github.com/hardrave/Commissure); same wording in a Devpost search excerpt). It runs unquantized Gemma-3-27B (about 55 GB of weights) by splitting the layers across three Cloud Run services, each on an NVIDIA L4 GPU in europe-west1. The services pass bfloat16 activations over gRPC, and Stage A exposes an OpenAI-compatible `/v1/chat/completions` endpoint ([README](https://github.com/hardrave/Commissure/blob/main/README.md)).
- **Sponsor tools and depth:** very deep on the platform: Cloud Run GPU, Cloud Build, Artifact Registry, Cloud Storage volume mounts for the weights, Secret Manager, IAM and Cloud Logging. A one-command CLI (`./commissure up`) builds and deploys everything. The model is Gemma (Google's open model).
- **Autonomy:** not an agent. It is infrastructure.
- **Demo style and video:** the README demo is a `curl` call streaming tokens. Video unknown (unverified).
- **Human or emotional story:** none. It uses a metaphor instead ("In neuroscience, a *commissure* is the bridge between the brain's hemispheres").
- **Live URL:** none (`https://<stage-a-url>` placeholder).
- **Team:** 1. All 6 commits are by hardrave, dated Nov 6 to 11, 2025 ([commits](https://github.com/hardrave/Commissure/commits/main)).
- **Why it most likely won (my reading):** it does something the product team would want to show: running a model larger than one L4 card's memory, while staying serverless. It is original (Innovation 20%), scores high on Technical Implementation, and is reproducible with one command.

## Patterns

1. **Each category winner did exactly what its category asked, in plain view.** AI Studio: an AI Studio export using many Gemini features. AI Agents: ADK `SequentialAgent` plus `LoopAgent` with 4 named agents. GPU: three L4 services running a 27B Gemma. The grand prize went to an ADK multi-agent app with a consumer audience.
2. **Agents with named roles, shown in a diagram.** Visionizer, Quest Creator and Illustrator; A1 Hazard to A4 Refiner; Stage A, B and C. Every repo I read has an architecture diagram or an ASCII flow, which matches the "architectural diagram" wording in the Demo and Presentation criterion (40%).
3. **Bonus points were easy to collect, and winners collected them.** All six repos I read use Google models. Five of the six describe two or more Cloud Run services; Rehearsal shows one Cloud Run back end, and its front end seems to run in AI Studio (unverified). At least two winners (PermitScan, SketchRun) published a build blog post.
4. **Low to moderate autonomy, with a human in control.** No winner takes real-world actions on its own. The agent-category winner drafts, checks itself in a loop, cites evidence, and then waits for a human "Approve". Storytopia adds a safety check before generating anything for children.
5. **The story is about who it helps, not a named person.** Kids and screen time (grand prize), worker safety (agents), the fear of making mistakes in a new language (AI Studio). I found no winner telling a named personal story (the videos could not be checked).
6. **Small teams.** Four of the six winners with a known repo show a single committer (Commissure, PermitScan, Rehearsal, SketchRun). The grand prize was a team of 2, and the largest known team (Piatto) was 4.
7. **Short visible build histories.** Five winning repos have a first commit 1 to 7 days before the Nov 10 deadline (Piatto, with 504 commits, is the exception), with README polish on the last day (Storytopia's commits include "Devpost Thumbnail" and "Project Architecture").
8. **Live links for apps, not for GPU projects.** Storytopia, PermitScan, Rehearsal and Piatto list a hosted URL (two Cloud Run front ends, one AI Studio app, one custom domain). The two GPU-category winners (Commissure, SketchRun) list none, probably because of GPU cost (my guess).
9. **Self-reported impact numbers are common** (PermitScan "99% time reduction", SketchRun "3-5x faster"). They read well, but none is independently backed.
