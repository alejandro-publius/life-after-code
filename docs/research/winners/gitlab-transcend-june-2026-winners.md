# GitLab Transcend Hackathon, June 2026: the 8 winners, read from their repos

Checked 2026-10-06 for Alex Velazquez ("Life After Code", October 2026). Sources: the public GitLab REST API (projects, trees, files, commits, pipelines) and the public GraphQL API (issue and MR notes, CI job status, AI Catalog items). Devpost, YouTube and about.gitlab.com are blocked here, so every winner below is marked "deep-dived: repo yes, page and video blocked". Counts are as of 2026-10-06. "Flow runs" means pipelines with source `duo_workflow` (one per Duo flow session). Test counts are rough (a regex count of test functions).

## How June was judged

GitLab's own status note (mmichaux-ext, 2026-07-13, in [team-task#1137](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/developer-relations/contributor-success/team-task/-/work_items/1137), re-read through GraphQL on 2026-10-06):

> "Each eligible submission was packaged into a self-contained packet (write-up plus an evidence bundle pulled from the actual repo code) and scored by three models in independent sessions: **Opus 4.8, GPT 5.5, and Gemini 3.1**. Around 800 scoring runs, tracked to a completion gate so nothing was skipped."

> "**The AI was decision support, not the decision.** Consensus gave clear shortlists for Technological Implementation and Quality of the Idea. It could not settle Potential Impact (models cap it differently) or Design (110 of 265 had silent or missing demos), so the panel resolved both by hand. Humans picked every winner."

> "Most submissions clustered into a few crowded problem spaces (69 built pre-merge impact review, 36 built onboarding aids), but the field still produced genuinely novel work: 17 one-of-a-kind concepts and 20 two-of-a-kind. Five of the eight winners came from those rare tiers."

The 2026-07-16 note in the same item adds: "We experienced some hurdles in Devpost platform and internally due to the not defined process." ([team-task#1137](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/developer-relations/contributor-success/team-task/-/work_items/1137))

The criteria, as one entrant copied them from the Devpost rules ([resource pack in transcend/3214092](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/3214092/-/blob/main/docs/transcend-hackathon-resource-pack-2026-06-20.md), a third-party summary, so treat the wording as unverified):

> "Technological Implementation: quality of software development, meaningful Orbit use, code quality, and whether the artifact works as described."
> "Design and Usability: ease of install, setup, interaction, and whether the workflow makes sense."
> "Potential Impact: whether the project solves a real software development lifecycle problem and gives other developers a useful pattern to copy."
> "Quality of the Idea: creativity, uniqueness, and whether it reveals a non-obvious Orbit use case."

Same source: "Submission period: June 10, 2026 at 10:00 AM EDT to June 24, 2026 at 2:00 PM EDT" (18:00 UTC); required "A story: developer pain point, how the agent/flow/skill fixes it, and what changes for the developer" and "A public YouTube or Vimeo demo video under 3 minutes"; "Each Showcase Track project can win at most one cash category prize"; first place $2,000 and second place $1,000 in each category.

## 1. Sankofa (Technological Implementation, 1st)

deep-dived: repo yes, page and video blocked

- **Repo:** [gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711), by @rogerkorantenng. Matched by the README title "Sankofa - Orbit-Powered Developer Intelligence Suite" and by catalog items such as flow [1011404 "Sankofa Unified"](https://gitlab.com/explore/ai-catalog/flows/1011404/), whose project is this repo.
- **What it does:** "Sankofa uses GitLab Orbit's knowledge graph to automatically surface structural code intelligence at three critical moments in the developer lifecycle": Radar (merge request blast radius), Guide (a knowledge brief when an issue is assigned), Shield (vulnerability propagation) ([README](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/blob/main/README.md)).
- **Orbit and Duo Agent Platform use (deep, broad):**
  - 3 agents ([agents/](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/tree/main/agents)) and 4 flows ([flows/](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/tree/main/flows)), each flow `version: v1`, `environment: ambient`, one `AgentComponent` with a local prompt. The "Unified" flow routes by keywords to Radar, Guide or Shield ([unified_flow.yml](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/blob/main/flows/unified_flow.yml)).
  - Orbit through `run_command` and `glab orbit remote query`, with JSON queries of four types: traversal, neighbors, aggregation, pathfinding ([agents/radar.yml](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/blob/main/agents/radar.yml)).
  - CI includes the `components/ai-catalog/catalog-sync@0.0.1` component for group 132494471 ([.gitlab-ci.yml](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/blob/main/.gitlab-ci.yml)); the GitLab CI bot then committed [.ai-catalog-mapping.json](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/blob/main/.ai-catalog-mapping.json) listing all 7 published items ([commit 5440e66d](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/commit/5440e66d)).
  - Triggers seen in practice: a new issue started Guide with no mention ([issue #2](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/work_items/2)); Guide posted briefs to [issue #1](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/work_items/1) "following pipeline success"; @mentions started Radar on [MR !2](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/merge_requests/2) and Shield on [issue #3](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/work_items/3).

The "Why" section uses statistics: "**63%** of developers spend >30 min/day searching for codebase context (SO 2023)" ([README](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/blob/main/README.md)).

The demo codebase came the same evening: "feat: add realistic application codebase for Orbit indexing" ([commit 750cba14](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/34570711/-/commit/750cba14)), seeded with a fake CVE story (issue #3 "CVE-2026-4521: MD5 collision vulnerability in crypto module").

## 2. Stayed Shipped (Technological Implementation, 2nd)

deep-dived: repo yes, page and video blocked

- **Repo:** [gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/2902648](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-ai-hackathon/transcend/2902648), by @sneg55. - **What it does:** "**Merged is not done. ## What won in June

Facts from the repos:

1. **Flow evidence split cleanly by category.** All 4 Technological Implementation and Design winners have successful flow runs and flow-made artifacts in their own repos (Sankofa 35, Stayed Shipped 3, Carver 2, Marshal 13). None of the 4 Impact and Idea winners has a single flow run (CrossCut, OrbitWeaver, Transcend, Universal Agent OS). See each section for links.
2. **Volume did not decide.** Non-winners ran far more flows (Contributor Compass 481, Semantic Bug Hunter 39, Downwind 35, CompatGuard 30) and published more catalog items (AFTERMATH 11, NEXUS 8) than most winners. Carver won Design with 2 flow runs and 8 commits.
3. **Winners were not flawless.** CrossCut has no runnable Duo flow and no pipelines; OrbitWeaver's demo links return 404 and its only pipeline failed; Universal Agent OS's flow file is not in the v1 shape and its catalog sync is a mock; Carver's runs used the committed manifest, not Orbit; Stayed Shipped's README status was stale; Stayed Shipped's and Marshal's flows both wrote into other entrants' projects.
4. **Solo builders and AI helpers were normal.** 7 of 8 repos show one human committer (CrossCut likely two, unverified). At least 3 winners show AI coding tools in commit metadata (Marshal: "Claude Code" author and Claude Sonnet 4.6 trailers; Stayed Shipped and Universal Agent OS: Claude Opus 4.8 trailers).
5. **Late and short builds won.** CrossCut's repo appeared 8 minutes before the deadline; Carver's repo was started on June 21 and OrbitWeaver's on June 23; Sankofa's whole history is one day (June 17).
6. **Two winners were repeat entrants** from the Feb to Mar 2026 AI Hackathon (Sankofa's and OrbitWeaver's authors), with earlier catalog flows.

Patterns (inference):

1. **Pick a job outside the two crowded clusters.** GitLab says five of eight winners came from the rare tiers. By my reading, Stayed Shipped (post-merge survival), Carver and Marshal (migration cost and migration campaigns, possibly a two-of-a-kind pair), Transcend (semantic reasoning) and Universal Agent OS (agent governance) are outside "pre-merge impact review" and "onboarding"; CrossCut (test selection) sits next to the pre-merge cluster; Sankofa and OrbitWeaver are blast-radius variants that won on build breadth and pitch. The crowded-space non-winners (Contributor Compass, Downwind, Tremor) had more activity than several winners.
2. **Be the best on one axis.** Each project could win only one prize. Sankofa won on breadth of platform use, Stayed Shipped on rigor, Carver on a one-command interaction, Marshal on a gated conversation, CrossCut and OrbitWeaver on a measurable saving, Transcend on novelty plus proof, Universal Agent OS on a new framing.
3. **For Technological Implementation and Design, leave run evidence in the repo.** The scoring packet used "an evidence bundle pulled from the actual repo code", and every winner in those two categories has flow-made issues, comments, commits or MRs in its own project. For Impact and Idea, the humans and models rewarded the idea and pitch even with no run evidence.
4. **Open with a plain job and a number or a person.** "estimates what a legacy migration will cost" with "human ≈$54k · gen ≈$10" (Carver); "38 minutes to 2 minutes" (CrossCut); "48 minutes", "3 of 12" missed (OrbitWeaver); a senior engineer "quietly repairing agent code" (Stayed Shipped); "'80% done' for six months" (Marshal); "a non-technical founder" (Universal Agent OS). This helps but is not enough: NEXUS had a strong story and did not win.
5. **Show the human control point.** 6 of 8 winners name one: Carver ("`main` is NEVER modified"), Marshal (`/approve` gates), Universal Agent OS ("Human approval is required"), Stayed Shipped (dry-run default, contest label), CrossCut (`/crosscut full` override), OrbitWeaver (MR for review). This lines up with October's theme, "Hands Off. How far can your agents go without you?" ([epic #40](https://gitlab.com/groups/gitlab-org/developer-relations/contributor-success/-/work_items/40), quoted in [gitlab_guide_and_reference.md](../gitlab_guide_and_reference.md)).
6. **Say what does not work.** Stayed Shipped calls itself "a one-sided alarm, by design"; Transcend publishes "Verified result: no"; Universal Agent OS has "Honest Status" and "Roadmap Honesty"; Marshal says "This boundary is stated openly"; Carver will "Refuse to invent numbers". Dead Code Finder and ReachGate did the same and did not win, so this is a habit of strong entries rather than a deciding factor.
7. **Demos with sound mattered most where humans judged.** 110 of 265 entries had "silent or missing demos" and Design was decided by hand; the one Design winner whose repo shows its demo plan (Marshal) planned "Screen recording with voiceover".

For Alex in October (inference): keep the flow inside your own project, make it run in the provisioned workspace so its outputs are in the repo, record a narrated demo, pick a job outside the crowded clusters, and state the human approval point plainly.
